## Selection approach

Each candidate was fetched and its response inspected. For readable HTML, the title, headings, main-topic content, relevant linked resources and available rights notices were reviewed. For PDFs, the actual downloaded format/signature and parser readability were checked, text presence assessed on every physical page, and representative pages inspected for structure. Long reports were sampled for semantic and layout review, not comprehensively checked claim by claim. HTTP failures prevented content, currency and document-specific permission verification; those entries remain unavailable rather than becoming approved because of a government domain.

Consumer household guidance is the default category. Clinical diets, scientific advice to policymakers, regulatory food-service rules, historical publications and discovery hubs are separately labeled. Government, institutional and editorial source identity remains separate from content role. Source dates retain their original labels, and URL path years, HTTP modification dates and retrieval dates are not assumed to be publication dates.

The final 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines and the 2025 advisory committee's scientific report are different publications. The report's own suggested citation says 2024, and it is advisory background rather than the final consumer policy. The new HHS fact sheets were linked by an official November 2026 announcement. Older NIH booklets remain valuable extraction assets, but their 2005/2006 dates prevent presenting them as newly issued guidance.

The Minnesota HTML/PDF pairs are one guidance family per topic. Cross-contamination and grilling PDFs show 2025 modifications despite older HTML revision labels. The storage PDF explicitly says July 21, 2006 even though its HTML host has a 2026 update. Compare the actual values, scope and conditions; never use the HTML update date to make the PDF appear current. The chart's columns and category rows require table-aware extraction: automatic detection missed this clearly visible table.

Selected content restrictions matter. The NHLBI sodium sheet contributes only general tips from physical page 1; its treatment claims and individual-food sodium table on page 2 are excluded. HHS adults/kids sheets contain calorie-calculator links, supplement passages and age-specific limits; their presence does not authorize personalized advice. Children’s guidance remains explicitly age-scoped. Image captions may be retained where rights allow, but copyrighted artwork is not automatically ingested with text.

## HTML currency observations

Visible Minnesota page updates range from January 2025 to September 2026, while printed HTML revision labels include April 2007 and August 2011. Those dates are recorded individually in the registry. Harvard’s structured metadata reports article publication dates in 2012/2014 and modifications in 2024/2025. Healthline reports original publication in 2021/2019 with later modifications; WebMD reports publication in 2021/2010 and review/modification in 2023/2024. These dates support a currency assessment but do not resolve reuse rights or establish that every medical claim is current.

NHLBI’s DASH HTML page shows an update in February 2026, and the targeted heart-foods page an update in December 2025. NIDDK’s visible CKD clinical review is January 2025 and diabetes review October 2023; their structured publication/modification fields show 2026 CMS dates. Keep those distinct rather than presenting a technical page date as a fresh clinical review. Unavailable candidates retain unverified dates, and generic locator pages may lack a document publication year. All dates are source-stated observations, not inferred from retrieval or URL paths.

## Usage and permission findings

- [HHS copyright policy](https://odphp.health.gov/copyright-policy) permits reuse of government information with attribution and a link; privately contributed images can require separate permission. No implied agency endorsement.
- The scientific report explicitly permits use and reprinting on physical page 2, with no implied HHS endorsement. Bibliographic citations do not license the cited third-party publications.
- [Cleveland Clinic terms](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/about/website/terms-of-use) explicitly restrict robots/scrapers and RAG/AI use without permission. Both relevant existing sources are retained as `blocked_usage` and disabled for ingestion.
- [WebMD terms](https://www.webmd.com/about-webmd-policies/about-terms-and-conditions-of-use) permit a single personal noncommercial copy; broader reuse for a RAG corpus was not established.
